# PART A — BUSINESS VISION AND SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE

## 1. Project Vision

Davejoe Interiors wants to create a premium, seamless digital customer and project-partner experience integrated directly into its existing Operations Management System.

The portal must support the complete relationship between Davejoe and its clients, from the first enquiry through quotation, contract acceptance, execution, quality verification, handover, reviews, aftercare and repeat business.

The portal must make communication transparent, preserve evidence, reduce disputes, support remote supervision and establish accountability.

It must protect both Davejoe Interiors and its clients.

The portal must not be a standalone website or complaint form. It must share data with the Company's CRM, project management, estimation, technical/QC, site reporting, procurement, logistics, finance and management dashboards.

## 2. Supported Customer and Project Types

The system must support five configurations:

*A. B2C Residential:* Individual homeowners, landlords and private clients engaging Davejoe directly.

*B. B2C Diaspora / Remote:* Homeowners and investors outside Nigeria who need detailed remote visibility of their projects.

*C. B2B Direct Corporate:* Companies, banks, offices, hotels, schools, hospitals, property developers and other organisations contracting Davejoe directly.

*D. B2B Subcontracting:* Main contractors, interior design companies and construction firms engaging Davejoe for painting, screeding, POP, drywall, special effects or other finishing packages.

*E. B2B Multi-Party Projects:* Projects involving property owners, main contractors, consultants, architects, engineers, project managers, Quantity Surveyors, facilities teams and several subcontractors.

These are configurable experiences within one system, not five separate applications.

Remote monitoring must also be available to B2B customers.

## 3. Core Principles

- One customer account may contain multiple projects.
- Each project has a unique ID and secure workspace.
- Each project may contain multiple work packages and areas.
- Every approval must identify the authorised approver.
- Significant decisions must be recorded and version-controlled.
- All client-facing progress claims must reflect verified information.
- Client complaints must reach appropriate Management independently of site personnel.
- Staff must see only information relevant to their roles.
- The Executive Director — Business Operations & Strategy and Managing Director/CEO must have comprehensive authorised oversight.
- The portal must be simple for clients and rigorous behind the scenes.
- It must work well on smartphones and slower internet connections.
- It must preserve historical records even after project closure.

# PART B — CUSTOMER ACCOUNTS, PROJECTS AND ACCESS

## 4. Customer and Organisation Accounts

Each customer must have a central profile containing:

- Individual or company name.
- Verified contact details.
- Email and WhatsApp number.
- Preferred communication channel.
- Billing information.
- Authorised representatives.
- Associated projects.
- Historical quotations and contracts.
- Invoices and payments.
- Aftercare history.
- Referral records.
- Communication preferences.

For corporate customers, the system must support multiple contacts with different authority levels.

For example, a designer may approve a colour but not additional expenditure. A Quantity Surveyor may review a variation but not authorise payment.

## 5. Multiple Projects and Returning Customers

A customer must not need a new unrelated account whenever they engage Davejoe.

Examples:

- A homeowner paints a residence this year and renovates another property next year.
- A bank engages Davejoe for several branches.
- A main contractor awards Davejoe painting work on three different projects.
- A diaspora homeowner requests a touch-up six months after completion.

The same customer account should display the relevant projects.

Each project must maintain its own contract, specifications, documents, financial records, complaints and approvals.

Minor aftercare should link to the original project. Substantial new work should generate a new linked project.

## 6. Secure Access

Clients receive a secure invitation through official email or WhatsApp.

The portal should support simple, passwordless access where appropriate.

Requirements:

- Identity verification.
- Expiring or revocable invitations.
- Project-specific permissions.
- Secure sessions.
- Stronger authentication for sensitive approvals.
- Immediate access revocation.
- Protection against cross-client data exposure.
- Audit logs.

A project link alone must not provide unrestricted permanent access.

# PART D — PROJECT BRIEF, SPECIFICATIONS AND APPROVALS

## 10. Digital Project Brief

Every project must contain a structured Project Brief.

It should include:

- Project description.
- Contractual customer.
- Project location.
- Work packages.
- Approved scope.
- Rooms, floors, zones or work areas.
- Quantities and measurements.
- Surface preparation.
- Products and materials.
- Colours and manufacturer codes.
- Finishes.
- Primer/coating systems.
- Number of coats.
- Relevant drawings.
- Approved samples.
- Exclusions.
- Schedule.
- Important technical notes.

Clients must be able to review the approved brief.

## 11. Room-by-Room and Area-by-Area Specifications

For homeowners, display specifications by room.

For commercial projects, display by floor, zone, room, elevation or work package.

Example:

*Kitchen*
- Approved colour: Yellow.
- Brand: Dulux.
- Colour code: [Actual code].
- Finish: [Specified finish].
- Number of coats: [Approved specification].
- Status: Approved.

*Floor 6 — Ceiling*
- Work package: POP and painting.
- Drawing reference: [Reference].
- Approved finish: [Specification].
- Status: Awaiting technical clearance.

The system must support photographs, sample references and drawing attachments.

Screen colour previews must not be presented as exact substitutes for physical samples.

## 12. Approval and Revision Control

Each specification must have:

- Version number.
- Prepared by.
- Date prepared.
- Relevant approver.
- Approval status.
- Date approved.
- Supporting documents.
- Revision history.

Statuses should include:

Draft → Submitted → Awaiting Approval → Approved → Superseded / Rejected.

An approved specification must not be silently edited.

A later change must create a new version while preserving the original.

## 13. Pre-Work Surface Inspection

Technical/QC must record:

- Existing substrate condition.
- Screeding/plastering quality.
- Cracks.
- Dampness.
- Unevenness.
- Damage.
- Photographs and videos.
- Recommended correction.
- Scope responsibility.
- Cost implications.
- Client/main-contractor decision.
- Clearance to proceed.

Where another contractor performed defective preparation, Davejoe must document observable defects and notify the appropriate authorised party.

A decision to proceed despite identified limitations must be recorded.

Such acknowledgement does not excuse Davejoe's own defective workmanship or authorise unsafe work.

# PART E — VERIFIED PROGRESS, EVIDENCE AND AREA PROTECTION

## 14. Project Progress Monitoring

The portal must provide:

- Current project stage.
- Planned milestones.
- Completed activities.
- Work in progress.
- Upcoming work.
- Pending client decisions.
- Delays.
- Technical concerns.
- Verified progress.
- Relevant photographs and videos.

Progress must distinguish between:

2. Inspected by Technical/QC.
3. Verified as completed.
4. Acknowledged by authorised client/main contractor.
5. Formally handed over where applicable.

These statuses must not be combined into one vague “completed” label.

## 15. Area Completion, Condition Evidence & Handover Control

*This is a mandatory core module for every project type, especially multi-contractor projects.*

Davejoe frequently completes painting or finishing work before other contractors have finished their own activities.

Other trades may subsequently damage completed walls, ceilings, screeding, POP or finishes.

The system must provide dated, structured evidence of the condition of Davejoe's work before another trade enters or before the area is released.

### 15.1 Area-Based Completion Record

Every project should support individual area completion records.

Examples:

- Living Room.
- Master Bedroom.
- Floor 2 Corridor.
- Floor 6 Conference Room.
- Floor 8 POP Ceiling.
- Staircase A.
- External Elevation B.

Each record should contain:

- Project ID.
- Work package.
- Area/location.
- Approved specification version.
- Date work was reported completed.
- Work performed.
- Quantities where relevant.
- Number of coats or applicable finish details.
- Site Supervisor.
- Assigned team.
- Completion photographs.
- Completion videos where appropriate.
- Technical inspection.
- Snag status.
- Verification outcome.
- Date/time of verification.
- Client/main-contractor acknowledgement.
- Handover status.

### 15.2 Completion Evidence Workflow

1. Site Supervisor marks an area as ready for inspection.
2. Supervisor uploads clear photographs and relevant evidence.
3. Technical/QC reviews the work.
4. Technical/QC records pass, conditional pass or failure.
5. Failed work generates a snag or corrective action.
6. Verified work is submitted to the appropriate client/main-contractor representative for acknowledgement where required.
7. The system records acknowledgement, comments, rejection or pending response.
8. The area may then be released or handed over according to the governing project procedure.

The developer must distinguish internal verification from external acknowledgement.

### 15.3 Evidence Standards

Evidence should include:

- Wide-angle photographs identifying the area.
- Close-up photographs of relevant finishes.
- Date and time information.
- Photographer/uploader identity.
- Area reference.
- Specification reference.
- Relevant inspection notes.
- Supporting video where necessary.

Where possible, preserve original file metadata.

A timestamp generated during upload must not be misrepresented as the time the image was captured.

Evidence must be stored securely and protected against unauthorised alteration.

### 15.4 Area Release to Other Trades

The system should support a status such as:

*“Davejoe Work Verified — Area Released for Subsequent Trades.”*

This may be used when Davejoe has finished its assigned work and other contractors need access.

The record should show:

- Condition at release.
- Outstanding snags, if any.
- Date of release.
- Receiving representative where applicable.
- Any agreed protection measures.
- Access or coordination notes.
- Whether formal contractual handover has occurred.

Area release must not automatically mean that all contractual risk or responsibility has transferred.

### 15.5 Subsequent Damage Reporting

If another contractor damages completed work, Davejoe personnel should be able to create a *Subsequent Damage / Rework Incident*.

The record must include:

- Original completion record.
- Earlier photographs.
- Date the damage was discovered.
- New photographs.
- Description of damage.
- Location.
- Known or suspected cause.
- Relevant contractors or activities.
- Witness information where available.
- Technical assessment.
- Proposed correction.
- Cost/time implications.
- Notification to the authorised customer/main contractor.
- Response and final resolution.

The system must not automatically declare another contractor liable merely because damage was discovered after Davejoe completed its work.

Responsibility must be assessed using evidence and the governing contract.

### 15.6 Rework Classification

Rework must be categorised as:

- Davejoe workmanship defect.
- Client-requested change.
- Main-contractor instruction.
- Damage by another trade.
- Pre-existing substrate issue.
- Material issue.
- Accidental damage.
- Cause under investigation.
- Other.

The classification should be supported by evidence and authorised technical review.

### 15.7 Commercial Follow-Up

Where rework is outside Davejoe's original responsibility, the system should create a linked variation or claim record where appropriate.

It must capture:

- Original completed work.
- Damage evidence.
- Required remedial work.
- Labour and materials.
- Cost estimate.
- Programme impact.
- Contractual notice.
- Authorised approval.
- Execution and verification.

Where the issue is attributable to Davejoe's workmanship, the system should generate the appropriate corrective work order without automatically charging the client.

### 15.8 Example

Davejoe completes painting on Floor 2.

The Site Supervisor uploads completion photographs.

Technical/QC verifies the area.

The main contractor's authorised representative acknowledges the condition.

An electrical contractor later damages the wall while installing fittings.

Davejoe uploads photographs of the new damage and links them to the earlier completion record.

The Technical Officer reviews the evidence.

The appropriate main contractor representative receives a damage notice and proposed remedy.

If the repair is chargeable under the contract, the correct variation procedure is followed before execution, subject to any urgent protective work required.

This process creates evidence, improves coordination and helps prevent unfair allocation of rework costs.

## 16. Before-and-After Comparison

The portal should allow authorised users to compare:

- Pre-work condition.
- Work in progress.
- Verified completion.
- Condition at area release.
- Subsequent damage.
- Repaired condition.

Clients should be able to see the relevant evidence according to their permissions.

This feature is particularly useful for diaspora clients and corporate project managers.

- Before-work photographs.
- Progress photographs.
- Completed-area photographs.
- Short videos.
- Inspection evidence.
- Material-delivery evidence where relevant.

- Work completed.
- Work verified.
- Photographic highlights.
- Upcoming activities.

# PART G — B2B AND SUBCONTRACT PROJECT MANAGEMENT

## 22. Multi-Organisation Projects

A project may involve:

- Property owner.
- Main contractor.
- Davejoe Interiors.
- Architect.
- Engineer.
- Quantity Surveyor.
- Project Manager.
- Facilities Manager.
- Other subcontractors.

Each organisation must have appropriate project access.

The end property owner must not automatically see Davejoe's subcontract prices, margins or commercial negotiations with the main contractor.

## 23. Contract Authority Matrix

For every B2B project, record who can:

- Issue technical instructions.
- Approve drawings.
- Approve samples.
- Request changes.
- Approve variation costs.
- Approve time extensions.
- Verify completed work.
- Certify payment.
- Accept handover.

A request from an unauthorised person must not automatically become an approved work instruction.

## 24. Work Packages

Support multiple work packages such as:

- Painting.
- Screeding.
- POP.
- Drywall.
- Special effects.
- Wall coverings.
- Flooring.

Each work package should have its own scope, specifications, quantities, programme, site reports, technical inspections, variations, payment records and completion status.

## 25. Requests for Information (RFIs)

Davejoe must be able to raise formal RFIs where drawings, specifications or responsibilities are unclear.

Each RFI must contain:

- Reference number.
- Question.
- Related area/work package.
- Drawing references.
- Supporting evidence.
- Recipient.
- Submission date.
- Response.
- Status.
- Potential cost/time impact.

An RFI response must not automatically be treated as a paid variation approval.

## 26. Site Instructions

Distinguish:

- Informal observation.
- Client request.
- Technical clarification.
- Formal site instruction.
- Approved variation.
- Emergency safety instruction.

Site Supervisors may receive instructions but must record and escalate them appropriately.

The portal must support external instructions from main contractors and their own project-management systems.

## 27. B2B Progress Claims and Certification

Support:

- Work-done quantities.
- Progress valuations.
- Supporting photographs.
- Inspection records.
- Payment applications.
- Certified amounts.
- Retention.
- Approved deductions.
- Outstanding balances.
- Final account records.

This information must connect to the finance dashboard.

# PART I — VARIATIONS AND CHANGE CONTROL

## 33. Additional Work Requests

The portal must support:

- Extra painting.
- Additional coats.
- Colour changes.
- Screeding repairs.
- POP alterations.
- Cleaning.
- Dismantling.
- Material removal.
- Additional installations.
- Other scope changes.

No employee or artisan may independently agree to additional paid work.

## 34. Variation Workflow

1. Request received.
2. Technical assessment.
3. Scope and quantity verification.
4. Cost/time assessment.
5. Internal commercial approval.
6. Client/main-contractor approval under the governing contract.
7. Revised work instruction.
8. Execution.
9. Technical verification.
10. Financial reconciliation.

The system must preserve original and revised specifications.

For B2B contracts, support instructed-but-unpriced, disputed and pending variations where required by the contract.

## 35. Colour Change Example

Client approves dark grey.

Davejoe completes two coats.

Client requests light grey.

The system retains the original approval and completion evidence.

Technical/QC assesses the additional work.

A revised quotation is issued where appropriate.

The authorised client accepts before the revised work is executed.

If Davejoe applied the wrong colour against the approved specification, the issue must instead be investigated as a potential workmanship or execution defect.

# PART J — SITE CONDUCT AND COMPANY CONTROLS

## 36. Unauthorised Work and Payments

All personnel must comply with Company policies prohibiting unauthorised:

- Private jobs connected to assigned projects.
- Direct payments for Company work.
- Commercial negotiations.
- Material removal or resale.
- Specification changes.
- Client diversion.
- Concealment of complaints.
- Misuse of Company resources.

The system should provide workers with a way to report improper requests.

## 37. Operational Roles

*Admin:* Client registration, quotation dispatch, communication intake, follow-up, approved mobilisation and administrative coordination.

*Technical/QC:* Surface inspections, technical assessments, specification verification, defect investigation and quality certification.

*Site Supervisor:* Assigned workers, site reporting, progress evidence, work instructions, material requests and incident reporting.

*Logistics/Procurement:* Approved material sourcing and movements, with appropriate separation of permissions.

*Accounts:* Invoices, reconciliation, approved payments and financial reporting.

*Artisans/Subcontractors:* Assigned tasks and approved work instructions only.

*Managing Director/CEO and Executive Director — Business Operations & Strategy:* Comprehensive authorised executive oversight.

# PART K — HANDOVER, REVIEWS AND AFTERCARE

## 38. Handover

- Snag list.
- Corrective actions.
- Area completion evidence.
- Client/main-contractor acknowledgement.
- Required completion documentation.
- Final financial reconciliation.
- Administrative closeout.

## 39. Written and Video Reviews

At an appropriate closeout stage, invite clients to submit:

- Written reviews.
- Video testimonials.
- Private feedback.
- Referrals.

Participation must be voluntary.

Clients must not be forced to provide a positive review or video before receiving handover documents or legitimate aftercare.

## 40. Testimonial Appreciation Offer

Davejoe may offer a future-service incentive, subject to Management approval.

Proposed example:

A 10% discount on the labour component of one future eligible service for a qualifying voluntary video testimonial.

The offer must have clear eligibility, expiry, exclusions and a maximum discount cap.

It must not depend on giving a positive rating.

Any published incentivised testimonial should be appropriately disclosed.

## 41. Aftercare

- Warranty inspections.
- Touch-ups.
- Repairs.
- Maintenance.
- Additional work.
- New projects.

The system should classify the request and link it to the original project.

Where appropriate, a new linked project should be created.
